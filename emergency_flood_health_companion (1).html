Trench Foot & Skin Infections

Prevention & Wound Care
Critical Warning: Never sit with wet boots/socks for long hours. Floodwater carries extreme pathogens including Leptospira bacteria.

Treatment & Prevention Protocol:

  • Dry Immediately: Wash feet/wounds with clean, disinfected water and soap. Dry thoroughly, especially between toes.
  • Elevate & Air: Keep feet elevated when resting. Air dry for at least 8 hours a day if possible.
  • Open Wounds: Apply iodine or antiseptic ointment. Cover with a waterproof bandage. Do not submerge open wounds into floodwater.
  • Fungal/Bacterial Rash: Keep clean, apply antifungal powder or antibacterial lotion if in kit.

Emergency Water Purification

Disinfection Ratios
Boiling Bring to rolling boil for 1 full minute (3 mins at high altitudes). Kill all pathogens.
Bleach Ratio Add 8 drops (1/8 tsp) of unscented household bleach per 1 Gallon of clear water. Wait 30 mins.
  • Cloudy Water: Filter through clean cloth/t-shirt or let settle before boiling or adding bleach (double bleach to 16 drops if cloudy).
  • SODIS (Solar Disinfection): Fill clear plastic bottles, lay in direct sunlight on metal roof for 6 hours (2 days if cloudy).

Leptospirosis, Cholera & Typhoid

Symptoms & First Response

Leptospirosis: High fever, severe calf muscle pain, red eyes, yellow skin. Action: Start early antibiotics (Doxycycline if available per health team protocol). Avoid wading in floodwater.

Cholera / Acute Diarrhea: Watery stool, dehydration, rapid pulse. Action: Continuous Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORS).

DIY Emergency ORS Recipe:

Mix 1 Liter clean water + 6 level teaspoons Sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon Salt. Drink continuously.
